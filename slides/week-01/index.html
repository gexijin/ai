Each click: everything highlighted so far is the context. The model gives every possible next word a probability, then picks one at random, weighted by those probabilities. The pick (amber) is appended and becomes part of the context. Step 3: "other" is not the most likely word, yet it was picked. Rewards guide changes to the model’s learned weights.

RL means reinforcement learning. Keep this explanation to about one minute. During training, the model generates replies and receives rewards. Reinforcement learning updates its internal learned weights to make higher-reward replies more likely. In reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), people compare replies, a reward model learns those preferences, and its scores guide the language model’s updates. Show Before RL first, then click to reveal After RL and the takeaway. Reuse the temperature slide’s words and numbers: remaining, other, next, missing. Before RL uses its Normal column (40%, 35%, 12%, 9%); After RL uses its Low column (60%, 38%, 1%, 0%). Here those distributions illustrate a possible training-induced change at a fixed temperature. RL does not necessarily concentrate probabilities this way and is not equivalent to lowering temperature. Rewards apply to replies, not a universal list of good or bad words. The bars show output probabilities, not individual neural-network weights. The percentages are illustrative, not measurements from a real RL training run. Only four candidates are shown; omitted words and rounding account for the remaining probability. The context and temperature are held fixed. On the next slide, temperature changes the sampling odds without retraining the model or updating its learned weights. RL training is a separate process from an ordinary chat. Source: Ouyang et al. (2022), Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback, https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155 .

Temperature controls how adventurous LLMs are

predictable ↔ varied

Same step as before ("...predict the ___"). Temperature does not change what the model predicts (Normal column); it changes the odds used when picking a word. Low: the top words take almost everything; answers are predictable and repetitive. High: the odds flatten; answers vary more and can drift off track. Analogy: a poet after a couple of beers knows the usual word but is more willing to pick a strange one: more creative, and also more erratic. Lower temperature is not more accurate: the most likely word is not necessarily true. Chat apps set temperature for you. Developer tools (OpenAI Playground, the API) let you change it for some models, but many newer reasoning models fix it and do not accept a different value. Numbers are the slide-10 probabilities at temperature 0.3, 1, and 2.

LLMs can use tools

The model can request a tool, receive its result, and use that information when preparing a response. Search finds information; a calculator does arithmetic; running code can compute or test something. The surrounding application runs the requested tool and returns its output to the model. This exchange can repeat. A chatbot needs access to the relevant tools for this to happen; a confident answer alone does not show that a tool was used. Keep the explanation brief.

LLMs always tell you what you want to hear (sycophancy)

Cheng et al., Science, 2026

Sycophancy: a tendency to tell the user what they want to hear, which can feel validating while not being true. Already named at the pushback demo (slide 32); this slide gives it data. Cheng et al., “Sycophantic AI Decreases Prosocial Intentions and Promotes Dependence” (arXiv 2510.01395; published in Science). Eleven leading models endorsed user actions roughly 40–55% more often than crowdsourced human responses did, including in “Am I the Asshole”-style posts where the community had judged the poster wrong. In two preregistered experiments (N=1,604), people who got sycophantic responses felt more justified, were less willing to repair the conflict, and were more likely to want to use the model again — the engagement payoff for the product. Note the figure's model list and percentages are the study's own measurements, not independently reverified here.

A second, distinct pattern from sycophancy: products can be designed to maximize engagement and time spent, independent of whether any one reply is sycophantic. The complaint itself alleges OpenAI designed ChatGPT to be “addictive, deceptive, and sycophantic.” Mention autism only as a reported fact about the plaintiff, never as a cause — do not imply autism made him more susceptible.
